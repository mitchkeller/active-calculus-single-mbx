Activity 1.6.10.

The position of a car driving along a straight road at time \(t\) in minutes is given by the function \(y = s(t)\) that is pictured in the following graph. The car’s position function has units measured in thousands of feet. Remember that you worked with this function and sketched graphs of \(y = v(t) = s'(t)\) and \(y = v'(t)\) in Preview Activity 1.6.2.
🔗

This graph shows a position function that passes through the following key points: \((0,0)\text{,}\) \((0,0)\text{,}\) \((1,2)\text{,}\) \((2,4)\text{,}\) \((3,4)\text{,}\) \((4,6)\text{,}\) \((5,8)\text{,}\) \((7,8)\text{,}\) \((8,10)\text{,}\) \((9,12)\text{,}\) \((10,12)\text{,}\) \((11,14)\text{,}\) and \((12,16)\text{.}\) The position function never decreases, and on the intervals \(2 \lt t \lt 3\text{,}\) \(5 \lt t \lt 7\text{,}\) and \(9 \lt t \lt 10\) is constant. In between those intervals, the function rises more and more quickly on the first half of the interval, and then rises less and less quickly on the second half of the interval. The overall graph is smooth and without sharp corners.
